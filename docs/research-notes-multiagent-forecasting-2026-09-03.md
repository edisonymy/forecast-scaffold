# Multi-agent research-sharing for forecasting bots: evidence memo

Question: for a Metaculus tournament bot, is it worth building agents that **share research and message each other** before pooling, versus (a) fully independent runs pooled at the end, or (b) one shared dossier with independent reasoning? Below: what's *measured* vs *asserted*, with sources.

## 1. Human forecasting literature

**Delphi rounds.** Panelists give anonymized point/probability estimates *plus written rationales*; a facilitator aggregates, anonymizes, and recirculates (including outlier reasoning) across 2+ rounds. Variance shrinks and panels are reported as more accurate than unstructured discussion — but this is documented mostly through case studies and methods texts, not controlled forecasting-tournament RCTs. [otexts.com/fpp3/delphimethod.html](https://otexts.com/fpp3/delphimethod.html) (2021); [casrai.org/guides/delphi-method](https://casrai.org/guides/delphi-method).

**Tetlock/GJP teams and superforecasters.** Individual superforecasters averaged Brier ≈0.166 vs ≈0.259 for regular forecasters; statistically aggregating forecasts within each group improved both further (≈0.146 vs ≈0.195). The superforecaster median beat other research teams by 35–72% and beat intelligence-community analysts (with classified access) by ~30%, and GJP's aggregate beat an internal IC prediction market by 25–30%. [goodjudgment.com/resources/the-superforecasters-track-record](https://goodjudgment.com/resources/the-superforecasters-track-record/the-first-championship-season/) (2015); [aiimpacts.org, "Evidence on good forecasting practices from GJP"](https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/) (2019). Mellers et al. (2014, *Psych Science*) reported teaming, training, and tracking each improved accuracy, superadditively when combined — **but** a 2025 reanalysis (Hauenstein, Thomas, Illingworth & Dougherty, *Psych Science*, [10.1177/09567976241266481](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09567976241266481)) refit the same tournament data with an item-response-theory model controlling extraneous variables and found the teaming/training effects were "substantially eliminated, reduced, and, in some cases, even reversed." **The team-effect size is therefore contested, not settled.** Mellers et al. (2015, *Perspectives on Psych Science*) found a combined dispositional+situational+behavioral model (open-mindedness, political knowledge, training, teaming, deliberation time, belief-updating frequency) reaching multiple R=.64 (R²≈.41); the AOT-alone effect size was not isolated in what we could verify — **mark unverified**.

**Prediction markets vs. team aggregation.** Atanasov et al. (2016, *Management Science*, [10.1287/mnsc.2015.2374](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2015.2374)), n>2,400 forecasters / 261 events: markets beat the simple mean of independent poll forecasts, but **team-based polls with statistical aggregation** (temporal decay, performance-weighting, recalibration) outperformed markets — a measured result, not an assertion.

**Herding / correlated error.** Lorenz, Rauhut, Schweitzer & Helbing (2011, *PNAS* 108(22):9020–9025, [pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1008636108](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1008636108)), N=144: showing others' prior estimates converged the group and shrank variance **without improving mean accuracy** — "remarkably little" social influence was needed to trigger this. This is the core mechanism risk for any "agents message each other" design.

## 2. LLM multi-agent literature

**Multi-agent debate.** Du et al. (2023/ICML 2024, [arXiv:2305.14325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325)): multiple LLM instances propose and debate answers over rounds; beats single-model CoT/reflection on 6 reasoning/factuality/QA tasks, black-box-model compatible. **ReConcile** (Chen et al., ACL 2024, [arXiv:2309.13007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13007)): round-table across diverse models (ChatGPT/Bard/Claude2) with confidence-weighted voting, +7.7% over single- and prior multi-agent baselines, beats GPT-4 on some datasets; model *diversity* was reported as the critical ingredient, not debate length. Both draw on the "Society of Mind" framing but neither targets calibrated probabilistic forecasting specifically — their gains are on reasoning/QA accuracy.

**Forecasting-specific work.** Schoenegger et al., "Wisdom of the Silicon Crowd" (*Science Advances* 10(45), 2024, [arXiv:2402.19379](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.19379v1)): a 12-LLM ensemble (independently run, then pooled) on 31 questions was statistically indistinguishable from a 925-person human crowd over 3 months; exposing GPT-4/Claude2 to the *median human forecast* improved those models' individual accuracy 17–28%, and simple human+machine averaging beat either alone — evidence for pooling/aggregation, not for agent-to-agent messaging. Schoenegger et al., "AI-Augmented Predictions" ([arXiv:2402.07862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07862), ACM TIIS 2025): giving *human* forecasters an LLM assistant improved their accuracy even when the assistant was deliberately noisy/overconfident — augmentation, not multi-agent debate. Halawi et al., "Approaching Human-Level Forecasting with LMs" ([arXiv:2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18563), 2024): single-agent retrieval→summarize→reason pipeline approaches but falls short of expert-crowd accuracy — no multi-agent messaging.

**Closest published architectures to "shared research + messaging."** AIA Forecaster (Lightning Rod Labs, Nov 2025, [arXiv:2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07678v1)): **M agents each do independent research** (not shared) and produce an initial forecast; a **supervisor** agent then reconciles disagreements, runs *targeted* follow-up search, and issues the final number. Reported on FB-7-21: single forecast Brier 0.1182 → mean-of-10 0.1140 → supervisor-reconciled 0.1125; backtested overall Brier ≈0.108 vs. superforecaster median ≈0.111 (self-reported, single-lab, not independently replicated). Note this is **critic/reconciliation, not peer debate** — agents never see each other's reasoning. The Bayesian Linguistic Forecaster ("Agentic Forecasting using Sequential Bayesian Updating of Linguistic Beliefs," [arXiv:2604.18576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18576), 2026) similarly runs K *independent* trials per question and combines them via logit-space shrinkage + hierarchical Platt calibration — statistical pooling, not messaging. We found no published forecasting paper testing a true "shared evidence pool with agents that discuss before finalizing" condition against independent-pooled and dossier-only baselines; this gap is itself notable. General debate literature also warns the mechanism can misfire: diagnostic work on "Confident Liar" dynamics ([arXiv:2606.10296](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10296)) finds confidently-wrong agents can sway correct ones toward agreement — debate raising *consensus*, not *calibration*.

## 3. What top Metaculus bots actually do

Fall 2025 FutureEval bot-maker survey (n=39; 29 winners/10 non-winners; [metaculus.com/notebooks/43337](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/43337/fall-2025-futureeval-survey/)) correlations with performance: **self-critique/red-team** r=−0.07 (p=.675, ns); **aggregates multiple forecasts** r=−0.19 (p=.236, ns); **research sources used** r=+0.42 (p=.006, sig, winners avg. 1.75 vs. 1.00 sources); **explicit base-rate calc** r=+0.38 (p=.032). Qualitatively, **multi-personality aggregation** was reported as ineffective — one winner: "multiple personas were a mistake... the final-stage LLM was bad at being genuinely critical," matching an internal Metaculus n=30 test finding "little to no improvement." The companion Bot Advice notebook ([metaculus.com/notebooks/43357](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/43357/bot-advice-fall-2025/)) contains an unattributed, uncorroborated claim that "debate is very powerful" alongside the multi-persona failure report — **mixed and not resolved by the quantitative survey**.

Architecturally, no top bot documented here uses free-form peer debate as its primary mechanism. **Panshul42** (Q2 2025 winner): a 5-agent "committee" (2× Sonnet, 2× o4-mini, 1× o3) runs in parallel over *shared scraped research* but forecasts independently, then combines via weighted averaging — shared-dossier-plus-lenses, not messaging. **nostreambot** (top open-source, 9th Fall 2025): explicitly includes Grok as a "low-correlation member" and uses median aggregation — deliberate decorrelation, not discussion. **Cassi AI** (#1 ForecastBench mid-2026) and **AIA Forecaster** both use an ensemble-then-reconciler-LLM pattern (supervisor/critic). **futuresearch.ai** and **laertes/Preseen** rank at or near the top of FutureEval/ForecastBench; their marketing pages reference "multi-agents" and "research teams" without architectural detail — **unverified**. No public design detail was found for **GreeneiBot2**, **mmBot**, **SynapseSeer**, or **acm_bot** beyond leaderboard placement — **unverified, no data**.

## 4. Synthesis

| Design | What's shared | Effect on error correlation | Evidence for accuracy gain | Cost multiplier | Main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Independent-pooled | Nothing until final vote | Lowest | Schoenegger 2024 ensemble ≈ human crowd; AIA mean-of-10 0.1182→0.1140 | ~N× (5–10×) | Diminishing returns past ~10 runs; common training-data blind spots still shared |
| Shared dossier + independent lenses | One research pass only | Moderate (single point of factual failure) | Panshul42 won Q2 2025 this way; but survey shows "research sources" (r=+0.42 sig) matters more than "personas" (~no effect) | ~1× research + N× reasoning | Bad/missing evidence propagates identically to every "lens"; personas often restyle, not truly disagree |
| Shared evidence pool, independent judgment (blackboard) | Raw findings, not conclusions | Lower than dossier-only, higher than fully independent | No clean forecasting RCT found isolating this condition — largely untested | ~1–2× research + N× reasoning | Anchoring on an early salient (possibly wrong) finding in the pool |
| Delphi rounds, anonymized numbers | Aggregated anonymized estimates + rationales, 2+ rounds | Deliberately raised (by design) | Case-study evidence of convergence/accuracy in expert panels; no GJP-scale RCT | 2–4× (rounds) + facilitation | Lorenz-style convergence without correctness; groupthink on round-1 modal answer |
| Free-form debate (agents see all reasoning) | Everything: transcripts, probabilities, confidence | Highest | Du et al./ReConcile: gains on reasoning/QA, **not** shown for calibrated forecasting; self-critique r=−0.07 ns in Metaculus survey | Highest (multi-round × N agents) | Confident-but-wrong agent sways others (arXiv:2606.10296); one Fall 2025 winner called it "a mistake" |
| Supervisor/critic over independent agents | Final forecasts + reasoning, to one reconciler only | Low–moderate | AIA Forecaster 0.1182→0.1125; Cassi uses same pattern (#1 ForecastBench) | ~N× + small reconciliation overhead | Supervisor becomes single point of bias; evidence is single-lab, self-reported, unreplicated |

**Recommendation.** (1) The paired test worth running is **independent-pooled vs. supervisor/critic-over-independent-agents** — it's the only interactive design with any forecasting-specific quantitative evidence (AIA, Cassi) and adds cost cheaply on top of an ensemble you likely already run. (2) Do not build free-form peer debate/messaging as the core mechanism first — no forecasting-specific calibration evidence exists, one direct field report calls it a failure, and the general debate literature documents confidence-driven consensus rather than accuracy. (3) The decision rule must score **both** Brier/log-score **and** the spread/error-correlation across your independent agents (pre-pooling forecast variance, or residual correlation) each run. (4) A variant only "wins" if Brier improves *without* forecast variance collapsing faster than the error shrinks — collapsing variance with flat/worse Brier is Lorenz's herding signature reproduced in-bot. (5) Run any comparison on a held-out question set the design wasn't tuned on, since every quantitative result above (AIA, Panshul42, nostreambot) is self-reported by the team that built the system.
